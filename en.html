Overview

A specialized center for electrical-network studies and technology

The Electrical Networks Research Institute began its activities in 1400 (Solar Hijri) at Shahid Beheshti University. It was established to participate in studies and technology related to electrical networks and to respond to the country’s research needs through specialized centers.

Direction

Institute objectives

  1. Cooperation in power-industry research projects.
  2. Providing technical and engineering services to different sectors of the power industry.
  3. Localization and cooperation in transferring the technical knowledge required by the country.
  4. Guidance and support for dissertations and theses useful to Iran’s power industry.
  5. Training specialist and skilled personnel in graduate programs and holding short specialized courses.
  6. Publishing effective power-industry research through the scientific journal “Research and Technology in the Power Industry.”

Education project

Reviewing and defining specialist transmission and development courses through knowledge management

The specialist electricity-course system prepared by universities and industry experts in 1382 SH had not been updated in line with rapid technological change and no longer met the needs of companies and learners. Tavanir authorized the definition of situational courses; however, their number often exceeded the approved courses, creating inconsistencies in company training calendars and budgets.

Project structure

  1. Steering committee: Tavanir representatives and project executors providing guidance, removing obstacles, facilitating expert cooperation and approving interim and final reports.
  2. Specialist subcommittees: Groups aligned with transmission-sector roles, contributing problems, solutions, current-state analysis, target-state definition and gap analysis.

Delivery phases

  1. Phase one: Review the current courses and occupational, organizational, technological and strategic needs.
  2. Phase two: Define the target state, analyze gaps and select learning strategies.
  3. Phase three: Review course titles, objectives, syllabi, content, delivery style, duration and prerequisites.
  4. Phase four: Prepare interim reports, steering-committee review and the final report.
